## evals
- [T2] OpenAI's lesson 1 is "Start with evals." Morgan Stanley ran translation, summarization and human-trainer evals, comparing model output with expert advisors. OpenAI reports that "98% of Morgan Stanley advisors use OpenAI every day; access to documents has jumped from 20% to 80%." — OpenAI, "AI in the Enterprise" https://cdn.openai.com/business-guides-and-resources/ai-in-the-enterprise.pdf (2025) · confidence: high
- [T3] Morgan Stanley timeline: the pipeline took 6–8 weeks to build, then about 4 more months of pilots, advisor labelling and eval refinement before trust was established. The ZenML summary's "98% adoption" matches OpenAI's own figure (**possible echo**). — ZenML LLMOps DB https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/forward-deployed-engineering-bringing-enterprise-llm-applications-to-production (2025) · confidence: medium
- [T2] Anthropic: "20-50 simple tasks drawn from real failures is a great start". Each task should be one where "two domain experts would independently reach the same pass/fail verdict", with a reference solution. Capability evals start at a low pass rate, and regression evals should be "nearly 100%". — Anthropic, "Demystifying evals for AI agents" https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents (2026-01-09) · confidence: high
- [T2] The same post: use pass^k (every trial succeeds) for customer-facing consistency. A 0% pass rate on a frontier model usually means a broken task or grader. Opus 4.5 went from 42% to 95% on CORE-Bench after grading bugs were fixed. — Anthropic https://anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents (2026-01-09) · confidence: high
- [T2] Anthropic's criteria guidance favours multidimensional success criteria, such as "F1 score of at least 0.85… on a held-out test set", "99.5% of outputs are non-toxic" and "95% response time < 200ms", with targets measured against a baseline. — Claude docs, "Define your success criteria" https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/test-and-evaluate/define-success (accessed 2026-10-17) · confidence: high
- [T4] Husain and Shankar report spending "60-80% of our development time on error analysis and evaluation". Start with "100 diverse traces", use binary labels, and appoint a single domain-expert "benevolent dictator". Generic metrics "create false confidence." — Hamel Husain, "LLM Evals FAQ" https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/ (2026-09-18) · confidence: medium
- [T4] At Nurture Boss, three issues caused more than 60% of failures. Focused work on date handling raised success from 33% to 95%. Roadmaps should count experiments run, not features shipped. — Hamel Husain, "A Field Guide to Rapidly Improving AI Products" https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/field-guide/ (2025-03-24) · confidence: medium
- [T4] Collect "at least 50-100 failures out of 200+ total samples". Align the LLM judge on 75% of the labels and test it on the held-out 25%, using precision, recall and Cohen's kappa (above 0.7 is excellent). Build "one evaluator per dimension." — Eugene Yan, "Product Evals in Three Simple Steps" https://eugeneyan.com/writing/product-evals/ (2025-11) · confidence: medium
- [T4] OpenAI's posting measures FDE success by "production adoption, measurable workflow impact" and "eval-driven feedback that changes product and model roadmaps". — OpenAI FDE Zurich https://jobs.thrivecap.com/companies/openai/jobs/87143008-forward-deployed-engineer-zurich (2026-07-22) · confidence: high
