# Ch 5: Discovery (`discovery`)

Part II · The Engagement / 第 II 部分 · 一次交付 · phase-num 5 · 3,500–4,500 words.

## p.goal draft
> The problem a customer names is a guess about their own business, and it is usually wrong. Discovery finds the lever leadership actually cares about and the workflow where it lives — on site, before anyone picks a model.

(First sentence: 20 words.)

## STEPs
**5.1 The wrong problem is the most common failure.** RAND, from 65 practitioner interviews, found that stakeholders who "misunderstand — or miscommunicate — what problem needs to be solved" was root cause #1, raised spontaneously in more than half of interviews (T1). Jarvis: "what the customer describes in scoping doesn't match the data/system reality" (T3). The stated problem is a hypothesis.

**5.2 Start with the business lever, not AI.** OpenAI's engagements open with leaders setting AI aside and naming business levers (T3, TNW). Palantir's projects "often start with an open ended question like 'Why are we delaying so many flights?'" (T4). Glean: "Before you write a line of code, you go deep on the business" (T4).

**5.3 Go where the work happens.** The European semiconductor engagement: the team "embedded on site, spent like a couple of weeks just like understanding their business", then chose verification, at 70–80% of engineer time, over the obvious target (T2, Altimeter video). Qureshi on tacit knowledge (T4). Sierra mapping real journeys with 20+ stakeholders and no APIs (T2). Palantir strategists "locate their biggest pain points" (T4).

**5.4 What to observe and ask.**
- Shadow the workflow.
- Get volumes and time as ranges.
- Find who owns the data and the decision.
- Find what "better" means to each stakeholder, and what the customer fears most.
- Spot the gap between the org chart and the real process.

**5.5 Composite (beat 2).** Two weeks on site. Adjusters spend roughly 30–50% of each day triaging incoming claim documents. The chatbot request was a proxy for "claims take too long". Patterns: RAND #1; the semiconductor discovery; Jarvis on data/system reality.

**5.6 Template: discovery interview guide.** Blank, then filled. Sections:
- the business lever;
- the current workflow, step by step;
- people and handoffs;
- volumes and time (ranges);
- data: where it lives, who owns it, how to get access;
- stakeholder definitions of better;
- the feared failure;
- the summary box: stated problem / observed problem / evidence / the lever it moves.

## Failure signals
- Discovery done by email.
- A model chosen in week 1.
- Only executives interviewed.
- No observed workflow.
- A single stakeholder's definition of better.

## Ready-to-move-on checklist
- [ ] Observed problem written and confirmed by the sponsor.
- [ ] Workflow mapped by watching it.
- [ ] Data owner named.
- [ ] Baseline measurable.
- [ ] Each stakeholder's "better" recorded.

## Closing tip
Write "stated vs observed" on one page and get the sponsor to sign the observed version before scoping.
