# Ch 13: When Engagements Fail (`when-engagements-fail`)

Part II · The Engagement · phase-num 13 · 3,500–4,500 words.

## p.goal draft
> Engagements rarely die suddenly: they fail for five named reasons that show weeks ahead, and the FDE who reads them early can still rescue the work or walk away cleanly.

First sentence: ≤30 words. Takeaway: five causes, early signals, three outcomes (call, rescue, walk), a decision rule written before you need it.

## STEPs
**13.1 What this phase is for, and how it fails.**
- Not a phase on the calendar; a decision that can arrive in any phase.
- Five recurring reasons: wrong problem, no data, no owner, moving sponsor, unagreed threshold. Map to RAND causes #1 and #2 plus leadership-driven failures.
- How failure handling itself fails: the slow fade (nobody calls it), the late kill, the blame post-mortem.
- The honest gap: no public first-hand account of an FDE deliberately killing an engagement.

**13.2 The composite (beat 10).** Months 6–9. Property expansion stalls when the COO's priorities shift to a cost programme. Signals: steering meetings slip, no property threshold owner, property labelling stalls. Pressure to cut adjuster roles on auto numbers (Klarna/CBA counter-case). Post-mortem in month 8; reset offer with deadline; no owner named by the date, so the vendor walks from property; auto stays in production, owned by the insurer. Cite RAND priority switching; Stanford same-sponsor; Klarna/CBA.

**13.3 Read the base rates without being fooled by them.**
- RAND (T1, interviews): 65 practitioners; 84% leadership-driven; "80%" is "By some estimates" (Fortune).
- MIT NANDA (T2): 95% sentence; method 52/153/300+; "directionally accurate"; success = deployment beyond pilot with KPIs, ROI 6 months post-pilot; Futuriom critique.
- Gartner (forecast, T3 as relayed): 30% abandoned after PoC by end 2025; 40% agentic cancelled by 2027, poll of 3,412 webinar attendees.
- Jarvis via TNW (T3): about 80% deployment.
- Lesson: base rates say failure is normal; they don't say which of yours will fail.

**13.4 Five failures, and the signal each sends early.**
- Wrong problem: MyCity (T1 testing; mayor's quotes reported).
- No data: RAND #2.
- No owner: Tryolabs? (not re-fetched; cut) → use Stanford semiconductor "no accountability for adoption" (T2).
- Moving sponsor: RAND "rapidly switch their priorities", "50 percent" quote (T1).
- Unagreed / unreached threshold: McDonald's low 80s vs 95%+ over ~2.5 years (T3).
- Plus the counter-case: success declared on an automation metric, then reversed: Klarna (T2 launch, T3 reversal; echo two-thirds vs 75%), CBA (T3).

**13.5 Call it: the mid-engagement post-mortem.** Run it while you can still act; no blame; facts against the terms you signed.

**13.6 Rescue or walk: the decision rule.** Five load-bearing conditions (sponsor who owns the number, threshold owner, data path, labelled set, a date). Rescue if the missing ones can be restored by a named person by a date; walk if not. Stanford: 61% of successes had prior failure; same sponsor; nobody punished (T2, draft, survivorship). Recruiting rescue: CEO owned, process fixed first, "I'm drowning".

**13.7 Walking away well.** Leave what works running and owned; hand over work-in-progress; write the trigger to come back; keep the sponsor.

## Template: engagement post-mortem (blank, filled)
Header, what we agreed (terms), what happened (timeline), the five conditions table, failure category, what still works and who owns it, decision (continue/reset/walk) with rule, what we hand over, the trigger to return, what goes back to product, signatures.

## Failure signals (threat-grid)
Sponsor cancels two steering meetings; threshold owner unnamed after 2 weeks; labelling stalls; "let's just keep going" with no date; headcount cut proposed on pilot metrics; nobody will sign the post-mortem.

## Ready to move on
- Decision written, with the rule it was taken under.
- Production system owned by the customer.
- Post-mortem shared with the sponsor, not just internally.
- Return trigger recorded.
- Product lessons filed.

## Closing tip
Write the walk-away rule into the pilot terms, before you need it.
