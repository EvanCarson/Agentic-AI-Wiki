Appendices

State of the Role

Every figure here will date, so use it as a snapshot, last verified October 2026, and lean on the chapters' arguments, which do not depend on any of it. A pay band or an open-role count quoted from this page next year may already be wrong; the reasoning in the chapter it came from will not be. Below, every fact from the book's Verified boxes, plus the dated market facts in the chapters' prose, is sorted by the question you are likely to bring: who hires and how many, what it pays in the US, Europe and China, which employers and deployment companies exist, what the titles and posts ask for, what the loops test, and what changed in 2025–26. The last section is the echo map: every number the book found circulating from a single source.

D.1

Reading this snapshot

Nothing on this page is new. Each fact is copied from a chapter with its date, its source link and its tier exactly as the chapter gives them, and each links back to the section that uses it, where the caveats and the argument live. Where a chapter's box gives no tier or no link, none has been added here.

Two reading rules carry over from How to Use This Book. First, the tiers: T1 is measured, primary and independent; T2 is measured and primary but vendor- or self-reported; T3 is secondary reporting; T4 is anecdote, opinion, advice, a stated target or a job post. Second, job posts: a post is primary evidence of what an employer asks for and advertises, and T4 evidence of how the job is actually done. Every posted band below shows what an employer advertised on the date given, not what anyone is paid.

Postings change and close without notice, and the counts move by the hour. Unless a row says otherwise, "read 17 October 2026" means read from the employer's own job-board feed on that day. Check a posting again before you quote it. Figures from different samples and windows sit side by side here only so you can find them: do not average them, and never blend growth rates across windows.

What to do.

  • Before you quote any figure on this page, open the source and re-date it; if the posting has closed or changed, say so rather than repeat the old number.
  • Quote a band with its post, its date and whether it is base or on-target earnings, and annualise Chinese offers by the number of months paid.
  • Keep growth figures and pay aggregates apart: one window and one sample per sentence, never an average across them.
  • When a number appears in a second outlet, check it against the echo map and count it once, at its origin.
  • Use the snapshot to choose questions, not conclusions; the questions to ask an employer are in Where FDEs Work, 15.8.
