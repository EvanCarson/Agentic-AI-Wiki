Templates

An engagement goes wrong in the blanks nobody filled in: the sponsor, the threshold, the owner after handoff. Here are the book's seventeen templates, blank and in chapter order, from the job check in Chapter 1 to the growth plan in Chapter 18. Each one is this book's proposal, not a standard. Copy it, cut what does not apply, and fill it in with names, dates and where each number came from.

B.1

Using these templates

The templates are in English in both editions on purpose, so the same sheet can go to a customer, a lead or a repository unchanged. Underscores mark a blank; fill it or say why it is empty, because a blank you cannot fill is usually the finding. Each section below says when to use the template, links to the chapter section that explains it, and describes the filled example there. Most examples come from the book's composite engagement, an insurer's auto claims triage, and carry ranges, not measured figures. No one has validated these forms against outcomes, so treat every line as advice (T4), cut what does not apply, and re-sign a template whenever its threshold, scope or authority changes.

B.2

Is this an FDE job?

Use it on a real posting before you apply, and again on the offer. It reads the post by stage and asks you to quote the text behind each answer: Y if the post says so, N if it says otherwise, ? if it is silent. A "?" is not a fail; it is a question for the first interview. Chapter 1, What an FDE Is, and Isn't, fills it in for a real posting chosen because it is hard to classify: Harvey's "Forward Deployed Finance Engineer, Buyout".

IS THIS AN FDE JOB?                 post: ____________  date: ______
Quote the post for every answer. Y = says so, N = says not, ? = silent

OWNERSHIP, DISCOVERY TO HANDOFF                          Y / N / ?
 1 Discovery: does the role find the problem, or get it   [ ]
   handed over on a ticket?  quote: ________________________
 2 Scope: does it agree scope and success criteria with   [ ]
   the customer?             quote: ________________________
 3 Build: does it write production code itself, or        [ ]
   design for a partner to build?  quote: __________________
 4 Production: does it stay until the system runs on      [ ]
   real traffic?             quote: ________________________
 5 Handoff: is "the customer runs it without you" named   [ ]
   as the end state?         quote: ________________________
 6 Loop: does field work feed the product, by a named     [ ]
   channel?                  quote: ________________________

SIGNALS THAT SHIFT THE READING
 7 Pay shape: base, or a base/variable split? ____________
 8 Team or job family: product / R&D / GTM / sales? _______
 9 Code: required, or "a plus"? __________________________
10 Who else is named alongside (AE, CSM, partner, PM)? ____

READING (take the first rule that matches)
 Mostly ? ............................ ask lines 1, 3 and 5 first
 N on 4-5 and a variable split ....... sales engineer, rebranded
 N on 3, or line 9 says "a plus" ..... strategist or architect:
                                       owns the problem, not the code
 N on 1-2 (problem arrives decided) .. contractor or delivery engineer
 N on 6 .............................. services; ask what happens to
                                       what you learn in the field
 1-6 all Y and code required ......... an FDE job, whatever the title

IF A LINE IS ?, ASK
 1 "Who decided what to build on your last engagement?"
 3 "Whose name is on the production commits?"
 4 "Where were you when it went live, and for how long?"
 5 "Who was running it three months after you left?"
 6 "Name one product change that came from the field."

B.14

Say-no note

What to do.

  • Before you apply, run the FDE-job check on the posting; at the offer, bring the question sheet to your future lead.
  • Fill in the engagement brief before signature, and treat every field you cannot fill as a finding, not a formality.
  • Send the data-access request to every approver on day one, and write the security answers before the customer's questionnaire arrives.
  • Keep the scope, the eval plan and the architecture records where the customer can see them, and re-sign them whenever a threshold, a set or a decision changes.
  • After each engagement, fill in the self-assessment and the growth plan, and ask your lead to strike anything they would not sign.
