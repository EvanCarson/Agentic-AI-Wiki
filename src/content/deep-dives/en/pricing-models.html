Deep Dive · Economics & ROI

Price the value the agent delivers, but only if you can survive the month its costs spike.

Agent products break the SaaS pricing playbook because cost of goods is real and variable — every "user" of the feature draws metered compute, so the seat-license intuition that minted 80–90% gross margins does not transfer. Picking a pricing model is choosing how price tracks delivered value and how much variable-cost risk you absorb versus pass through. Get the alignment right and growth is healthy; get it wrong and your best customers are your biggest losses.

STEP 1

The SaaS margin intuition does not survive metered compute.

Classic SaaS priced a seat against near-zero marginal cost, so usage and margin were decoupled and gross margins ran 80–90%. An agent product reintroduces COGS: each active use spends model and tool budget, and 2025–2026 operators report agent gross margins commonly landing in the 50–60% band rather than SaaS-like. Any pricing model that does not reckon with a real, variable, sometimes-spiking cost of goods is a SaaS model wearing an agent costume — it will look profitable until usage shifts.

STEP 2

Seat, usage, and outcome pricing each misalign somewhere.

The three models trade off predictability against value alignment. Per-seat is predictable for the buyer but decouples price from cost — a power user destroys the margin while a dormant seat subsidizes it. Usage-based (per token/call/run) tracks your cost well but charges for effort, including failures, which customers resent and which punishes them for your inefficiency. Outcome-based (per resolved ticket, approved lead, shipped asset) aligns price with value but transfers all execution-cost risk onto you. No model is free of misalignment; the choice is which misalignment you can manage.

STEP 3

Align price with value, then defend the floor.

Outcome pricing is where the market is heading in 2026 because it sells the thing the customer actually wants and makes ROI legible to them. But it only works if priced off fully-loaded cost per success at a realistic success rate, not the happy-path token cost.

# outcome price must clear loaded cost-per-win with a margin band
loaded_cost_per_win = (model + tools + retries + escalation + eval) / success_rate

# industry rule of thumb: 2.5x–3.5x the blended per-unit cost
outcome_price = loaded_cost_per_win * 3.0
# the multiple is the buffer for the p90 cost month, not greed

The 2.5–3.5× multiple over blended per-unit cost is not margin padding — it is the buffer that keeps you solvent in the 90th-percentile cost month, when retries, the long tail, and escalation all spike together. Price at 1.2× and you are insolvent the first bad month.

STEP 4

The more autonomous the agent, the more pricing risk you hold.

Autonomy and margin risk move together. A tightly-scoped agent on a short leash has predictable cost; a long-horizon autonomous agent can decide to spend 50× on a hard instance, and under outcome pricing that variance lands entirely on you. The more autonomy you sell and the more you price on outcomes, the more you are running an insurance book on the cost distribution — and an underpriced insurance book is profitable right up until the claims arrive.

STEP 5

Hybrid pricing places risk where each party can bear it.

Mature 2025–2026 packaging is rarely pure; it composes models so each covers a different risk:

  • Platform fee + outcome unit — the fee covers fixed cost and dampens variance; the unit aligns the upside with delivered value.
  • Outcome price with a fair-use cap — value alignment for the normal range, with a ceiling that contains the pathological tail you cannot afford to insure for free.
  • Usage pass-through above an included tier — you absorb predictable volume, the customer bears the marginal cost of their own heavy or abusive use.
STEP 6

When outcome pricing is the wrong promise to make.

Outcome pricing requires an objective, mutually-trusted definition of the outcome and a cost tail you can bound; if "success" is disputable, or the cost variance is unbounded and uninsurable, outcome pricing converts every hard case into a loss you contractually owe. Sell outcomes only where you can both verify the outcome and survive its worst cost month; otherwise a hybrid that caps your downside is honest pricing, not a weaker offer.
